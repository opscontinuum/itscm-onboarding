## APPENDIX J TEST AND MAINTENANCE SCHEDULE

- **Review frequency, the triggers, and the owner of the review**: Annually, at the same time as the tier-assignment workshop is re-run, and owned by the plan's director, who is the DR Commander and owns the annual review. Six triggers require re-signing rather than re-dating: any material assumption changing; any tier assignment changing; a drill failing to meet a tier's tolerable downtime where the finding is closed by changing the plan rather than the estate; after a real invocation and the subsequent failback; and the designated authority changing. The plan is careful about the limit of this: those triggers cover re-approval of the statement, which is one part of its own gap G3, and the plan-maintenance section that gap asks for is still open. Its record of changes says the same thing, that a review finding nothing to change produces no row and a row does not evidence that a review took place. Separate cadences do exist for testing, quarterly at minimum and monthly for tier 0, and for the roster, rung monthly with a row unanswered for ninety days treated as unreachable.

## Recorded for this plan

- **The route from a drill finding to a change in the plan**: For a drill there is a route and the plan states it: the drill produces a defect list with owners and due dates, and the measured recovery and work-recovery numbers are committed back over the design targets in the tier document, which the plan calls what turns this from a document into a programme. Every commit that changes plan content adds a row to the record of changes in the same commit, because a change without a row is a change the printed plan does not know about. A drill failure that is closed by changing the plan rather than the estate triggers re-approval. The risk and audit function receives the evidence and owns the plan-change route. For a real event there is no route, and the plan says so: it has no after-action report template, and records that there is no defined route from a finding to a plan change beyond the record of changes itself. No register of open findings exists.
